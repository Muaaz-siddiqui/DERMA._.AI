ECZEMA (Atopic Dermatitis)

What is Eczema?
Eczema is a chronic inflammatory skin condition that causes dry itchy and inflamed skin. It is very common affecting more than 10 percent of people worldwide. Eczema is not contagious and cannot be spread from person to person. It tends to flare periodically and then subside. Eczema often begins in childhood but can occur at any age and many children outgrow it. It is frequently associated with asthma and hay fever as part of the atopic triad.

Types of Eczema:
- Atopic dermatitis: Most common type, chronic and inflammatory
- Contact dermatitis: Caused by contact with irritants or allergens
- Dyshidrotic eczema: Affects hands and feet with small blisters
- Nummular eczema: Circular coin-shaped patches
- Seborrheic dermatitis: Affects oily areas like scalp and face
- Stasis dermatitis: Related to poor circulation in legs

Appearance:
- Dry scaly patches on skin
- Red to brownish gray patches
- Intense itching especially at night
- Small raised bumps that may weep fluid when scratched
- Thickened cracked or scaly skin
- Raw swollen skin from scratching
- Most commonly on hands feet ankles wrists neck upper chest eyelids and inside bend of elbows and knees
- In infants often appears on face and scalp

Causes:
- Genetic mutations affecting skin barrier function
- Overactive immune system response
- Environmental triggers including dust mites pet dander pollen mold
- Skin irritants like soaps detergents shampoos and cleaning products
- Stress and emotional factors
- Sweating and heat
- Certain foods in some cases especially in children
- Dry skin and disrupted skin barrier

Risk Factors:
- Family history of eczema asthma or hay fever
- Living in urban areas or developed countries
- Certain mutations in filaggrin gene affecting skin barrier
- Exposure to harsh chemicals or irritants at work
- Being born to older mothers
- Early antibiotic use in infancy

Symptoms:
- Intense itching that can be severe especially at night
- Dry sensitive skin
- Red inflamed skin
- Dark colored patches of skin
- Rough leathery or scaly patches
- Oozing or crusting
- Areas of swelling
- Scratching that leads to a cycle of more itching

Triggers to Avoid:
- Certain soaps shampoos and detergents
- Rough scratchy clothing especially wool
- Synthetic fabrics
- Rapid temperature changes
- Perspiration
- Stress
- Certain foods (eggs nuts milk in children)
- Dry air and low humidity
- Cigarette smoke

Treatment Options:
- Moisturizers: Apply thick creams or ointments several times daily
- Topical corticosteroids: Reduce inflammation during flares
- Topical calcineurin inhibitors: Alternative to steroids for sensitive areas
- Antihistamines: For itching especially at night
- Biologic medications: Dupilumab for moderate to severe eczema
- Phototherapy: Ultraviolet light therapy
- Wet wrap therapy: For severe cases
- Oral corticosteroids: For short term severe flares
- Antibiotics: If skin infection develops from scratching

Skin Care Routine:
- Bathe in lukewarm not hot water for 10-15 minutes
- Use gentle fragrance-free soap
- Pat skin dry gently do not rub
- Apply moisturizer within 3 minutes of bathing while skin is still damp
- Use fragrance-free hypoallergenic products
- Wear soft breathable cotton clothing
- Keep fingernails short to reduce damage from scratching

When to See a Doctor:
- Eczema interferes with sleep or daily activities
- Skin infection signs including yellow crusting increased pain warmth
- Rash is not improving with home treatment
- Rash is widespread and severe
- Fever develops with skin symptoms
- Child has severe eczema affecting growth or sleep

Prevention of Flares:
- Identify and avoid personal triggers
- Moisturize skin daily even when no flare present
- Use gentle skincare products
- Manage stress
- Keep home humidity at comfortable level
- Avoid overheating and sweating

Important Note:
Eczema is a chronic condition that requires ongoing management. A dermatologist can create a personalized treatment plan. Do not use steroid creams on your face without medical advice. Always consult a qualified dermatologist for proper diagnosis and treatment of eczema.
