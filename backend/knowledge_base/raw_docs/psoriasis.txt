PSORIASIS

What is Psoriasis?
Psoriasis is a chronic autoimmune skin condition that causes rapid buildup of skin cells. This buildup of cells causes scaling on the skin surface. Psoriasis is a long-term condition that tends to go through cycles flaring for a few weeks or months then subsiding. Psoriasis affects about 2 to 3 percent of the population worldwide. It is not contagious and cannot be spread from person to person. While there is no cure effective treatments are available to manage symptoms.

Types of Psoriasis:
- Plaque psoriasis: Most common type, causes dry raised red lesions covered with silvery scales
- Nail psoriasis: Affects fingernails and toenails causing pitting and abnormal growth
- Guttate psoriasis: Small drop-shaped lesions usually triggered by infection
- Inverse psoriasis: Affects skin folds causing smooth red inflamed patches
- Pustular psoriasis: Uncommon type with pus-filled blisters
- Erythrodermic psoriasis: Least common and most severe, covers entire body with red peeling rash
- Psoriatic arthritis: Psoriasis with joint inflammation

Appearance:
- Red patches of skin covered with thick silvery scales
- Dry cracked skin that may bleed
- Itching burning or soreness
- Thickened pitted or ridged nails
- Swollen and stiff joints in psoriatic arthritis
- Patches most commonly on elbows knees trunk and scalp
- Can affect any body part including genitals and scalp

Causes:
- Autoimmune condition where immune system attacks healthy skin cells
- T cells mistakenly attack skin causing rapid skin cell production cycle
- New skin cells develop in days instead of weeks
- Old skin cells do not shed fast enough leading to buildup
- Exact trigger for immune malfunction unknown but genetic and environmental factors involved

Risk Factors:
- Family history of psoriasis
- Viral and bacterial infections especially streptococcal throat infection
- Stress which can trigger or worsen flares
- Smoking
- Heavy alcohol use
- Vitamin D deficiency
- Certain medications including lithium beta blockers and antimalarial drugs
- Obesity
- Skin injury (Koebner phenomenon - psoriasis appearing at injury sites)

Common Triggers:
- Infections especially strep throat
- Skin injuries cuts burns insect bites
- Stress
- Smoking
- Heavy alcohol consumption
- Certain medications
- Vitamin D deficiency
- Cold and dry weather

Symptoms:
- Red inflamed patches of skin
- Silvery white scales on patches
- Dry skin that cracks and bleeds
- Itching and burning sensation
- Thick ridged or pitted nails
- Swollen tender joints
- Patches on scalp that may cause dandruff-like flaking

Complications:
- Psoriatic arthritis affecting about 30 percent of psoriasis patients
- Eye conditions like conjunctivitis and uveitis
- Cardiovascular disease increased risk
- Type 2 diabetes increased risk
- High blood pressure
- Metabolic syndrome
- Depression and anxiety due to skin appearance
- Crohn disease association

Treatment Options:
Topical treatments (mild to moderate):
- Corticosteroids: Most commonly prescribed
- Vitamin D analogues: Calcipotriene to slow skin cell growth
- Retinoids: Tazarotene to reduce skin cell production
- Coal tar: Reduces scaling itching and inflammation
- Salicylic acid: Promotes shedding of dead skin cells
- Moisturizers: Daily use to reduce dryness

Phototherapy (moderate):
- UVB phototherapy: Narrowband UVB most effective
- PUVA therapy: Psoralen plus UVA light
- Excimer laser: For localized patches

Systemic medications (moderate to severe):
- Methotrexate: Reduces skin cell production
- Cyclosporine: Suppresses immune system
- Biologics: Target specific immune pathways (adalimumab etanercept ustekinumab secukinumab)
- Small molecule drugs: Apremilast and tofacitinib

Skin Care for Psoriasis:
- Moisturize daily especially after bathing
- Take short lukewarm baths with mild soap
- Apply moisturizer immediately after bathing
- Avoid scratching as this can trigger Koebner phenomenon
- Use humidifier in dry weather
- Get safe amounts of sunlight but avoid sunburn
- Avoid known triggers

When to See a Doctor:
- First appearance of psoriasis symptoms
- Symptoms are severe or widespread
- Psoriasis does not improve with treatment
- You develop joint pain swelling or stiffness
- Psoriasis is affecting quality of life
- You develop signs of infection in psoriasis patches
- You are considering stopping or changing medications

Important Note:
Psoriasis is a chronic condition requiring long-term management. A dermatologist can create a personalized treatment plan. Psoriasis is associated with serious comorbidities like psoriatic arthritis and cardiovascular disease so regular medical follow-up is essential. Never stop medications without consulting your doctor. Always work with a qualified dermatologist for proper psoriasis management.
