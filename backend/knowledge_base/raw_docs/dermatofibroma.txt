DERMATOFIBROMA

What is Dermatofibroma?
Dermatofibroma is a common benign skin growth that most often appears on the legs but can occur anywhere on the body. It is a harmless fibrous nodule that develops in the deeper layers of skin. Dermatofibromas are more common in adults and are seen more frequently in women than men. They are completely benign and do not become cancerous.

Appearance:
- Small firm raised bump on the skin
- Usually less than 1 centimeter in diameter
- Color ranges from pink to red to brown to gray
- Surface may be slightly scaly
- Feels hard or firm when pressed
- Characteristic dimple sign: pinching the lesion causes it to dimple inward
- Usually a single lesion but multiple can occur
- Commonly found on lower legs

Causes:
- Exact cause is unknown
- May develop after a minor skin injury like an insect bite or ingrown hair
- Considered a reaction of fibroblasts (connective tissue cells) to minor trauma
- Not caused by infection or virus
- Not contagious

Risk Factors:
- Female gender (more common in women)
- Adults between 20 and 50 years old
- Minor skin injuries or insect bites
- Possible genetic predisposition
- Rarely multiple dermatofibromas can be associated with certain immune disorders

Symptoms:
- Firm nodule on skin usually on legs
- May be itchy or tender when touched
- Usually painless
- Dimples inward when pinched (diagnostic sign)
- Color may darken over time
- Grows very slowly or not at all

Diagnosis:
The dimple sign (Fitzpatrick sign) is characteristic of dermatofibroma. When the skin around the nodule is pinched between two fingers the lesion dimples inward. A dermatologist can usually diagnose this clinically but a skin biopsy can confirm the diagnosis if there is any uncertainty.

Treatment Options:
Treatment is usually not necessary as dermatofibromas are harmless:
- Observation: Most dermatofibromas do not need treatment
- Surgical excision: If the lesion is bothersome or diagnosis is uncertain
- Cryotherapy: Flattens but does not completely remove the lesion
- Laser therapy: Can help flatten raised lesions
- Shave excision: Flattens the lesion but may leave a scar

When to See a Doctor:
- Any new firm skin nodule to confirm diagnosis
- Lesion that grows rapidly
- Lesion that bleeds or ulcerates
- Lesion that is painful
- Multiple dermatofibromas appearing suddenly
- Any lesion you are unsure about
- If the dimple sign is not present as this may indicate a different condition

Difference from Dangerous Conditions:
Dermatofibroma must be distinguished from dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans which is a rare malignant tumor. Key differences include:
- Dermatofibroma is small and stable while DFSP grows larger
- Dermatofibroma shows positive dimple sign
- Any rapidly growing or large fibrous nodule needs immediate evaluation

Important Note:
Although dermatofibroma is benign any new or changing skin lesion should be evaluated by a dermatologist. Do not attempt to remove or cut skin lesions at home. Always seek professional medical evaluation for proper diagnosis and peace of mind.
