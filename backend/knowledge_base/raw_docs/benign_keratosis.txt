BENIGN KERATOSIS (Seborrheic Keratosis)

What is Benign Keratosis?
Benign keratosis, commonly known as seborrheic keratosis, is a common noncancerous skin growth. It is one of the most common benign skin tumors found in adults. These growths often appear in middle age and their number tends to increase with age. Despite their sometimes alarming appearance they are completely harmless and do not become cancerous. They can appear anywhere on the body except the palms and soles.

Appearance:
- Waxy scaly slightly raised growths on the skin
- Color ranging from light tan to brown to black
- Round or oval shaped
- Vary in size from very small to more than 1 inch across
- Have a rough texture that looks stuck on or pasted on the skin
- May look warty or have a rough bumpy surface
- Sometimes have small horn-like projections
- Can be single or appear in clusters

Causes:
- Exact cause is not fully known
- Strong genetic component as they run in families
- Age is a major factor as they become more common with aging
- Sun exposure may play a role in some types
- Not caused by viruses unlike warts

Risk Factors:
- Age over 40 years
- Family history of seborrheic keratoses
- Sun exposure may trigger some types
- Hormonal changes in some cases

Why They Are Not Dangerous:
Seborrheic keratoses are completely benign and do not transform into cancer. They are not contagious and are not caused by any infection. However they can sometimes be mistaken for melanoma or other serious skin conditions which is why medical evaluation is important whenever a new or changing skin lesion appears.

Difference from Dangerous Lesions:
- Benign keratoses do not change rapidly
- They have well-defined borders
- They are not asymmetrical in a concerning way
- They do not bleed spontaneously
- However any rapidly changing lesion should be evaluated by a doctor

Treatment Options:
Treatment is not medically necessary as these are benign. However removal may be desired for cosmetic reasons or if the lesion causes irritation:
- Cryotherapy: Freezing with liquid nitrogen
- Curettage: Scraping off the growth
- Electrocautery: Burning the growth with electric current
- Laser therapy
- Shave excision

When to See a Doctor:
- New growth that looks like seborrheic keratosis to confirm diagnosis
- Growth that bleeds itches or becomes irritated
- Growth that changes rapidly in size shape or color
- Any skin growth you are unsure about
- Multiple new growths appearing suddenly which can rarely indicate internal cancer (sign of Leser-Trelat)
- If you are worried or anxious about a skin lesion

Important Note:
Although benign keratoses are harmless it is important to have any new or changing skin lesion evaluated by a dermatologist to rule out malignant conditions. Never self-diagnose skin conditions as some dangerous lesions can look similar to benign keratoses. Always consult a qualified dermatologist for proper diagnosis.
