MELANOMA

What is Melanoma?
Melanoma is the most dangerous form of skin cancer. It develops in the melanocytes which are the cells that give skin its color. Although melanoma is less common than other skin cancers it is far more dangerous because of its ability to spread to other organs if not detected and treated early. Melanoma is curable when caught early but can be life-threatening when it spreads. Early detection is absolutely critical for survival.

Appearance:
- Often resembles a mole but with irregular features
- Can appear as a new unusual growth or a change in an existing mole
- May be brown black tan red white or blue
- Has irregular asymmetric shape
- Has uneven ragged or blurred borders
- Has multiple colors within the same lesion
- May be larger than 6mm
- May be raised or flat
- Can appear anywhere on the body including areas not exposed to sun
- In men most common on trunk (back and chest)
- In women most common on legs

Types of Melanoma:
- Superficial spreading melanoma: Most common type, grows outward before downward
- Nodular melanoma: Most aggressive, grows quickly downward into skin
- Lentigo maligna melanoma: Grows slowly, common in older people on sun-damaged skin
- Acral lentiginous melanoma: Occurs on palms soles and under nails, more common in darker skin

Causes:
- Ultraviolet radiation from sunlight damaging DNA in melanocytes
- Tanning bed use especially before age 35 increases risk by 75 percent
- Genetic mutations in BRAF NRAS and other genes
- Family history of melanoma
- Personal history of melanoma or other skin cancers
- Weakened immune system

Risk Factors:
- Fair skin light hair and light eyes
- History of sunburns especially blistering sunburns in childhood
- Excessive UV exposure from sun or tanning beds
- Living near equator or at high altitude
- History of dysplastic or atypical moles
- More than 50 common moles
- Family history of melanoma
- Personal history of melanoma
- Weakened immune system
- Certain inherited conditions like xeroderma pigmentosum

ABCDE Warning Signs (CRITICAL):
- A Asymmetry: One half does not match the other
- B Border: Irregular ragged notched or blurred edges
- C Color: Multiple colors including tan brown black red white or blue
- D Diameter: Larger than 6mm though can be smaller
- E Evolution: Any change in size shape color or new symptoms like bleeding

The EFG Rule for Nodular Melanoma:
- E Elevated: Raised above skin surface
- F Firm: Hard to touch
- G Growing: Increasing in size rapidly

Stages of Melanoma:
- Stage 0: Only in outer layer of skin, 99 percent survival rate
- Stage 1: Thin melanoma, 92-97 percent 5-year survival rate
- Stage 2: Thicker melanoma without spread, 53-81 percent survival rate
- Stage 3: Spread to lymph nodes, 40-78 percent survival rate
- Stage 4: Spread to distant organs, 15-20 percent survival rate

This is why early detection is so critical.

Treatment Options:
- Surgery: Primary treatment, excision of melanoma with margins
- Immunotherapy: Checkpoint inhibitors like pembrolizumab and nivolumab
- Targeted therapy: BRAF inhibitors for melanomas with BRAF mutation
- Chemotherapy: Less used now but still an option
- Radiation therapy: For melanoma that has spread to brain or bones
- Clinical trials: Access to newest treatments

When to See a Doctor IMMEDIATELY:
- Any mole showing ABCDE warning signs
- Any rapidly changing skin lesion
- New dark streak under a nail
- Any new unusual growth especially in high risk individuals
- Lesion that bleeds without injury
- Any skin change that concerns you

Do not wait. Melanoma can progress from early stage to advanced stage in weeks to months.

Prevention:
- Apply broad spectrum SPF 30 or higher sunscreen daily and reapply
- Wear protective clothing wide brim hats and UV blocking sunglasses
- Seek shade especially between 10am and 4pm
- Never use tanning beds
- Perform monthly self skin examinations
- Annual professional skin checks especially if high risk
- Protect children from sunburns as childhood sun damage significantly increases lifetime risk

Important Note:
Melanoma is a potentially life-threatening cancer. If you notice any suspicious skin lesion see a dermatologist immediately. Do not wait for your annual checkup. Early stage melanoma is highly curable but advanced melanoma is very difficult to treat. Regular skin self-exams and professional skin checks save lives. Always consult a qualified dermatologist or oncologist for melanoma evaluation and treatment.
