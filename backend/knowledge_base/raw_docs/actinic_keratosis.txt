ACTINIC KERATOSIS (Solar Keratosis)

What is Actinic Keratosis?
Actinic keratosis is a rough scaly patch on the skin caused by years of sun exposure. It is considered a precancerous condition meaning it can develop into skin cancer if left untreated. Actinic keratosis is very common especially in people over 40 who have had significant sun exposure throughout their lives. It most commonly appears on sun-exposed areas like face, lips, ears, scalp, neck, and hands.

Appearance:
- Rough dry scaly patch of skin usually less than 1 inch in diameter
- Flat to slightly raised patch on the top layer of skin
- Color ranging from pink to red to brown
- Itching burning or tenderness in the affected area
- Hard wart-like surface
- Dry cracked lips if on lips
- Appearance like a horn on skin in extreme cases

Causes:
- Cumulative sun exposure over many years
- Ultraviolet (UV) radiation from sunlight
- Use of tanning beds or sunlamps
- Damage to skin DNA from UV rays
- Weakened immune system

Risk Factors:
- Age over 40 years
- Fair skin that burns easily
- History of frequent or intense sun exposure
- Living in sunny or high altitude climates
- History of sunburns especially in childhood
- Weakened immune system due to disease or medication
- Personal or family history of actinic keratosis or skin cancer

Why It Is Dangerous:
Actinic keratosis is precancerous. If left untreated it can progress to squamous cell carcinoma which is a type of skin cancer. Studies show that up to 10 percent of actinic keratoses can develop into squamous cell carcinoma over time. Early treatment is essential to prevent this progression.

Treatment Options:
- Cryotherapy: Freezing the lesion with liquid nitrogen
- Curettage: Scraping off the lesion
- Topical medications: Fluorouracil cream, imiquimod cream, diclofenac gel
- Photodynamic therapy: Light therapy to destroy abnormal cells
- Laser therapy: To remove damaged skin
- Chemical peels for multiple lesions
- Excision: Surgical removal in some cases

When to See a Doctor Immediately:
- Any rough scaly patch that does not go away
- Lesion that bleeds or itches persistently
- Lesion that grows rapidly
- New skin growth that looks unusual
- Any skin change after years of sun exposure
- If you have had actinic keratosis before as recurrence is common

Prevention:
- Use broad spectrum SPF 30 or higher sunscreen daily
- Wear protective clothing including hats and long sleeves
- Seek shade especially between 10am and 4pm
- Avoid tanning beds completely
- Perform regular self skin examinations
- Get annual skin checks from a dermatologist if you are high risk

Important Note:
Actinic keratosis must be evaluated by a dermatologist. It is a precancerous condition and early treatment prevents skin cancer. Never ignore rough persistent patches on sun-exposed skin. Early detection and treatment are critical for good outcomes.
