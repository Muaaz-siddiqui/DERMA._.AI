ACNE (Acne Vulgaris)

What is Acne?
Acne is a chronic inflammatory skin condition that occurs when hair follicles become clogged with oil and dead skin cells. It is one of the most common skin conditions worldwide, affecting people of all ages but most commonly teenagers and young adults. Acne can appear on the face, chest, back, and shoulders.

Types of Acne:
- Whiteheads: Closed clogged pores
- Blackheads: Open clogged pores
- Papules: Small red tender bumps
- Pustules: Papules with pus at their tips
- Nodules: Large solid painful lumps under the skin
- Cystic lesions: Painful pus-filled lumps that can cause scarring

Causes:
- Excess oil (sebum) production
- Hair follicles clogged by oil and dead skin cells
- Bacteria (Propionibacterium acnes)
- Hormonal changes during puberty, pregnancy, or menstrual cycle
- Certain medications like corticosteroids
- Diet high in refined sugars and dairy
- Stress
- Genetics and family history

Symptoms:
- Whiteheads and blackheads on face, forehead, chest, shoulders
- Red tender bumps on skin
- Pus-filled pimples
- Large painful solid lumps under skin
- Painful cysts deep under the skin
- Oily skin
- Skin scarring after healing

Risk Factors:
- Age (teenagers most commonly affected)
- Hormonal changes
- Family history of acne
- Greasy or oily substances on skin
- Friction or pressure on skin from helmets or tight collars
- Stress increases cortisol which worsens acne

Treatment Options:
- Topical treatments: Benzoyl peroxide, salicylic acid, retinoids, antibiotics
- Oral medications: Antibiotics, birth control pills, isotretinoin for severe acne
- Procedures: Drainage, steroid injections, laser therapy, chemical peels
- Lifestyle: Gentle face washing twice daily, avoid picking or squeezing pimples
- Non-comedogenic moisturizers and sunscreen
- Diet modifications: Reduce dairy and high glycemic foods

When to See a Doctor:
- Acne does not improve after 2-3 months of self-care
- Acne is severe with many nodules and cysts
- Acne is causing emotional distress
- Acne is leaving permanent scars
- Over the counter treatments are not working
- Sudden severe acne in adults

Prevention Tips:
- Wash face twice daily with gentle cleanser
- Use oil-free non-comedogenic skincare products
- Remove makeup before sleeping
- Shower after sweating
- Avoid touching face with hands
- Stay hydrated and eat balanced diet
- Manage stress through exercise and sleep

Important Note:
Acne is a medical condition and a dermatologist can provide the most effective treatment plan. Do not pop or pick pimples as this causes scarring and spreads bacteria. Always consult a qualified dermatologist for persistent or severe acne.
